A · Gingham weave — five ways to run the check
A1 · Fine check

Baseline. 10px weave at low contrast, running everywhere. Needs a soft cream halo behind the copy — at this scale the check reads as static under type.

Stay in the loop.

New drops, pop-up dates, and the occasional love letter.

A2 · Big picnic check

Scaled to 30px with the contrast up. Confident and tablecloth-y. Squares are large enough to read as blocks rather than noise, so the type sits straight on it with no scrim.

Stay in the loop.

New drops, pop-up dates, and the occasional love letter.

A3 · Gingham hem

Check only along the top and bottom edges, like the hem of a tablecloth. The middle stays clean cream so nothing competes with the sign-up.

Stay in the loop.

New drops, pop-up dates, and the occasional love letter.

A4 · Cut on the bias

Same weave rotated 45°. Diagonal reads as fabric grain rather than graph paper and softens the grid’s rigidity — a sewing-room detail more than a pattern.

Stay in the loop.

New drops, pop-up dates, and the occasional love letter.

A5 · Dissolving check

The swing. Strongest weave of the set, masked so it fades to nothing behind the words. Full pattern at the edges, clean cream where the type lives — legibility without faking it.

Stay in the loop.

New drops, pop-up dates, and the occasional love letter.
