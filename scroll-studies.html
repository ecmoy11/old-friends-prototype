01 · The seam

One stitch, the whole way down.

Look at the left margin. A single running stitch is being sewn as you scroll, and it never stops — it runs the entire length of the page. Every section is a piece of the same seam, and a knot ties off wherever one section meets the next.

This is the one I'd fight for. It costs almost nothing, it's on the page the entire time rather than firing once and being over, and it says the thing the brand is actually about: everything here is one continuous piece of handwork.

A dashed gradient in a rail the height of the document. Scroll position sets the inner element's height — that's the whole mechanism. The knots are IntersectionObservers on the section boundaries.
02 · Uncovered, not faded in

Product photos lift like a cloth.

Right now the grid fades up, which is what every site does. These are wiped open from the bottom with a clip path instead, easing out of a slight over-scale so each one settles rather than arrives. Staggered a tenth of a second apart across the row.

Reads as a cover being drawn back off the piece. A fade reads as a browser loading something.

clip-path: inset(100% 0 0 0) → inset(0), plus scale 1.07 → 1, on a long ease-out. Triggered once by IntersectionObserver, never re-run.

03 · The wipe

Their fabric becomes your piece.

Scroll into the frame below and keep going. It pins, and the scroll drives a seam across the image, turning the fabric someone brought in into the thing Lauren made from it.

Of everything here this is the one that does real work. Commission is the hardest thing to explain in words and the easiest thing to show, and the visitor performs the transformation themselves with their own thumb.

A 260vh stage with a sticky 100vh child. Scroll progress through the stage sets one CSS variable, which drives the top image's clip-path and the seam's position.

04 · Layered cloth

Sections lay over each other.

Instead of scrolling past one another, each section pins and the next slides over the top of it with a soft shadow at its leading edge — the way you'd lay one piece of fabric over another on a cutting table.

Fair warning: this is the showiest thing on this page and the one most likely to wear out. I'd use it for exactly one handoff — probably hero into Core Collection — and nowhere else.

position: sticky; top: 0 on consecutive full-height sections. No JavaScript at all.

Everything here had a life before.

A jacket that went to somebody's first job. A tablecloth from a kitchen that isn't there any more.

We find them and mend them.

Sometimes as they were. Sometimes as something else entirely.

Then we send them out again.

Worn in, not out.

05 · The thread between the steps

How it works, sewn together.

The commission process is four numbered steps sitting in a row. A thread draws left to right between them as you scroll, so the four separate things become one sequence you're being walked through.

Same trick as the seam, turned ninety degrees: scroll progress across the section sets the width of a dashed gradient.

Where I'd start

One, three, then two.

The seam first — it's cheap, it's everywhere, and it's the only one that's a brand idea rather than an effect. The wipe second, on the commission page, because it sells the hardest-to-explain thing you offer. The uncover third, as a straight swap for the fade you already have.

Four is a party trick. Five is nice but small. And none of it should run for anyone who's asked their machine for reduced motion — every one of these is already wired to switch off if they have.
