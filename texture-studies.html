Texture studies — cloth, not color blocking

Every swatch below is ONE ink on a cream ground. No second color, no grid of blocks.
All twelve carry a woven-fiber grain (SVG turbulence, 20% multiply) — that’s the part that makes it read as fabric instead of pattern.
The library — twelve weaves, tonal only

Ordered roughly quietest to loudest. The first four are the ones I’d actually put behind type; the back half are stronger and want to be used in a strip or a single panel, not a full page.

01 · Plain-weave tooth

1px warp and weft on a 3px pitch, 9% brown. Below the threshold where your eye resolves squares — it just reads as slightly rough paper-cloth. The safest full-page ground here.

02 · Slub linen  ← my pick

The threads are deliberately uneven — thick, thin, thick, on different rhythms in each direction. That irregularity is the whole difference between “hand-loomed” and “CSS gingham.” Never resolves into a grid.

03 · Handloom weft

Horizontal threads only, uneven spacing. Kills the checkerboard problem outright because there’s no cross-axis to form squares with. Reads like the back of a woven runner.

04 · Herringbone

Real herringbone — opposing diagonal bands, 12px columns. Classic heirloom-textile move (think an old wool coat). Directional energy without any color contrast.

05 · Chevron twill

Herringbone’s cousin, drawn as a continuous line instead of blocks. Softer, a little more decorative. Nice as a thin band under a section header rather than a whole background.

06 · Basketweave, tonal

This is your reference image’s actual structure — over-under-over — with the four colors stripped out and one olive doing all the work. Same weave, none of the blocking. Worth comparing side by side with what you sent.

07 · Canvas duck

Heavier plain weave, 2px threads on a 5px pitch. Utility-bag texture. Would suit the tote and commission pages better than the shop grid.

08 · Grosgrain rib

Vertical only, with a light side to each rib so it looks raised. Reads as ribbon or corduroy. Good for a narrow vertical element — a sidebar, a nav rail, a label.

09 · Bouclé nub

Scattered slubs at three different tile sizes so the nubs never line up, over a faint weave. The most tactile of the set and the only one that isn’t on a strict axis.

10 · Waffle

Cell wall plus a lighter inner shadow, 11px cells. Honest warning: this one is closest to reintroducing a grid. Keep it under 12px and it stays cloth; scale it up and it becomes graph paper.

11 · Seersucker

Puckered bands in dusty blue over a fine all-over weave. The only swatch with a visible second tone — included so you can see where the line is. To my eye it’s still stripe, not block, but you may read it differently.

12 · Muslin scrim

Open weave, 7px gaps, dusty blue at higher opacity on the brighter cream. Airy and light. This is the closest to what’s on the site now, shown for comparison.

The grain is the whole trick — same slub linen, three levels

Left is the pure CSS weave with no noise layer. The middle is what every swatch above uses. The right is pushed hard. Without the grain it’s a diagram of cloth; with it, it’s cloth.

Grain 0%

Clean, mechanical, slightly cold. You can see the repeat.

Grain 20%  ← default

The repeat disappears. Enough fuzz to feel woven, still clean behind type.

Grain 42%

Properly rough — raw linen. Too much for body copy, right for a hero or a full-bleed break.
